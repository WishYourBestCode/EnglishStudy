AI and the job market

Artificial intelligence is replacing some jobs and creating others. How far do you agree? Give reasons and examples to support your opinion.

Write your own response ↓
Original practice essay

I agree that artificial intelligence will replace many jobs, but I also believe that it will create new career opportunities.

First, AI is changing the job market by automating repetitive tasks. For example, many companies now use AI to handle customer inquiries, analyze data, and generate reports. As a result, some employees may lose their jobs because businesses can complete these tasks with fewer workers.

Second, AI is creating new employment opportunities. For instance, companies need skilled professionals to develop, maintain, and improve AI systems. When I graduated from computer science in 2024, many of my classmates struggled to find traditional IT jobs, while others found opportunities in AI-related fields.

In conclusion, although AI may eliminate certain positions, it will also create new career paths. Therefore, I believe that people should continue developing their technical skills to remain competitive in the changing job market.

Exam-length model answer · 260 words

Consider a shop that receives hundreds of questions each week about deliveries. If a computer can answer most of them, the owner has less reason to hire another assistant. I agree that artificial intelligence will remove some jobs while creating others, although the people who lose work may find it difficult to move into the new roles.

Jobs are particularly vulnerable when most requests follow a predictable pattern. A customer who wants to know when a parcel will arrive needs a tracking update, which a program can retrieve in seconds. A customer whose parcel contains a broken wedding gift may need someone to arrange a replacement before the ceremony. The shop still needs a person with the authority to make that decision. Yet if only a few cases require such attention, one assistant might handle work that previously occupied three.

The same shop illustrates how new work could arise. Suppose its software repeatedly promises next-day delivery to customers on an island where the ferry runs twice a week. Someone must investigate the error, change the instructions and check future replies. A former assistant who knows the delivery routes could help a software developer spot similar mistakes. However, this opportunity would be of little use to that assistant if the employer required a computing qualification and offered no training.

For these reasons, I expect AI to change both the number of jobs available and the skills they require. Employers can ease the transition by giving experienced staff time to learn how to test the tools that will handle their former duties.
