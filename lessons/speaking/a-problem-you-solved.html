Speaking

A problem you solved

Practise a one- to two-minute IELTS Part 2 talk. Then give a reasoned answer to a Part 3 follow-up question.

Your cue card

Describe a time when you solved a problem. Say what happened, who was involved, what you tried, and how you felt about the outcome.

Set a timer for one minute. Write a few keywords below. When the minute ends, start speaking and try to keep going for up to two minutes. Leave the model closed for this first attempt. If you want to listen back, record your talk with your phone.

Read a model talk

Last winter I agreed to make lunch for a few friends who were helping me move. I promised them soup and fresh bread. I had switched the oven on twenty minutes earlier, but when I opened the door to put the bread in, it was still stone cold. My first thought was to buy bread, but the nearest bakery was closed on Sundays.

I rang my sister because she makes flatbreads in a frying pan. She told me to divide the dough into small balls, roll them out thinly and cook them one at a time. I tried one straight away. It burnt on the outside while the middle was still sticky, so I turned the heat down and made the next one much thinner. That worked. When my friends arrived, one person rolled out the dough while I cooked it. We ate the bread straight from the pan with our soup.

I’d been embarrassed about getting lunch wrong, especially since everyone was already doing me a favour. In the end, cooking together was the part people remembered. Now I test the oven before I invite anyone round.

Part 3 follow-up

When is it better to ask for help than to solve a problem alone? Speak for about 30–45 seconds. Explain your view and give an example where your answer would change.

Compare a possible answer

I’d ask for help quite early if getting something wrong could affect someone else. For example, if I smelled gas in a shared kitchen, I’d leave and call for help straight away. With something smaller, like a recipe that hasn’t worked, I’d have another go myself. I often remember a solution better when I’ve worked out why the first attempt failed.

Listen back

  • Could a listener tell why the problem mattered and how you solved it?
  • Did you use the past tense for the events and the present for a lesson you still follow?
  • Which detail made the story yours? Keep that detail and try the talk again using only your keywords.
