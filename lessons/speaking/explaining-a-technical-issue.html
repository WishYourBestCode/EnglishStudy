Speaking

Explaining a technical issue

Play the support technician. Help a colleague print a document, using words they can act on. With a partner, take turns playing each role. On your own, read each line aloud and pause to give your reply.

Take the call

Colleague: “I clicked Print twice, but nothing came out. I need this page for a meeting in five minutes.”

  1. Acknowledge the deadline. Ask one question to find out where the document was sent.
  2. Colleague: “The box beside ‘Printer’ says ‘Save as PDF.’ Is that the right one?” Explain what that choice does in one sentence.
  3. Guide them to the printer called Office East. Give one instruction at a time.
  4. Colleague: “Okay, I clicked Print. Am I done?” Ask them to check the result.
Read the model conversation

Colleague: “My meeting starts in five minutes, and nothing’s coming out of the printer.”

Technician: “Let’s check where the page is going. When you open the print window, what name do you see beside ‘Printer’?”

Colleague: “It says ‘Save as PDF.’ I thought I’d sent it to the printer.”

Technician: “That choice saves a copy on your computer. Click ‘Save as PDF’ to see the other choices. Can you see ‘Office East’ in the list?”

Colleague: “Yes, there it is.”

Technician: “Choose ‘Office East,’ then click Print once. Could you check whether the page has come out?”

Colleague: “I’ve got it. Thanks!”

Try a follow-up

Colleague: “I can’t see ‘Office East’ in the list.” Reply aloud in two or three sentences. Ask what they can see and offer a next step that takes the meeting deadline into account.

Compare a possible reply

“Which printers do you see in the list? If there’s another one nearby that you’re allowed to use, we can try that for the meeting. Then I can help you add ‘Office East’.”

Phrases to reuse

  • “What do you see beside …?” Use the label the person can read on their screen.
  • “That choice means …” Explain why the last action produced that result.
  • “Could you check whether …?” Ask for something the person can see or hear.
