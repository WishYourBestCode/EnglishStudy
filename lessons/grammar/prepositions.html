Grammar

Prepositions of time

Use at for clock times, on for days, and in for months. Since marks a starting point, for a duration, and by a deadline.

Compare the examples

  • The course starts in July, on a Monday, at nine.
  • I have worked here since May. I have worked here for six months.

Practice

Choose one answer for each question.

1. The meeting begins ___ 9:15.

9:15 is a specific clock time.

2. The library closes early ___ Fridays.

Fridays names a recurring day of the week.

3. The outdoor pool opens ___ June each year.

June is a month. Use in when no day is given.

4. The help desk has been open ___ three hours.

Three hours measures how long the desk has been open.

5. We have used this software ___ last Tuesday.

Last Tuesday marks the beginning of a period continuing to the present.

6. Friday at noon is the deadline. Please submit the form ___ then.

By then means at or before that time. Until describes how long an activity continues.
